What Astrodoc watches

Tiredness you can't feel

Astronauts sleep about 6 hours a night in space, though NASA schedules 8.5. Tired people rarely notice it. Astrodoc puts sleep, slower reactions and heart rate together and says so plainly.

In the app: the card at the top of the dashboard always shows the most urgent problem and one thing to do now.

A clot nobody felt

On the ISS, 6 of 11 crew members had slow or reversed blood flow in a neck vein, and one had a clot. It was found by ultrasound, not by symptoms. Astrodoc watches the blood tests for the same pattern.

In the app: when D-dimer rises while platelets fall, it says "possible blood clot", opens the checklist and sends the report itself.

Emergency signs, always first

Hard to breathe, chest pain, weakness on one side: any one of them comes before every other question.

In the app: press Check how I feel, tap the astronaut where it hurts, tick any emergency sign, and Astrodoc gives the steps and reports at top priority.

Radiation for the whole trip

NASA's Curiosity rover measured 1.8 mSv a day in deep space and 0.67 on Mars. Added up, a crew passes NASA's 600 mSv career limit while on Mars.

In the app: the radiation chart shows each person's line, today's dose and the day they pass the limit.

Bones that quietly thin

Bones lose 1 to 1.5% of their density each month on long missions. Astrodoc tracks six months of trends against NASA's numbers.

In the app: "The last 6 months" shows sleep, mood, bone and body mass, and says which need attention.

How to use Astrodoc

Four things, in the order you'll need them.

  1. Say who you are

    Choose Commander, Pilot or Mission Specialist once. Astrodoc opens on your own health, with your crewmates one tap away.

  2. Read the top card

    Every reading has one of three levels, always shown with a shape and a word:

    All good Watch Needs care now

    Do the "Do now" step first.

  3. Feel something? Check it

    Press Check how I feel, tap the 3D astronaut, answer a few yes-or-no questions, and see what it could be, what to do and why.

  4. Ask, or just talk

    Type or speak to Astrodoc: "How is everyone?", "My leg is swollen", "Calm me down". Reports go to Earth by themselves, and wait on board when the link is down.

Showing it to someone? Open Demo controls at the bottom of the dashboard to start a solar storm, cut the Earth link, or connect the Arduino pulse sensor.
