How it works

Three simple steps

1

Measure

A small sensor reads heart rate and temperature. The crew adds a daily check-in, a 1-minute reaction test and blood test results.

2

Check

Every reading is compared with limits from NASA research. Astrodoc also spots dangerous combinations, not just single numbers.

3

Act

If something is wrong, a warning appears with one clear action. A report goes to Earth, and the crew doesn't wait for the reply. Or just ask the voice assistant: "How is the commander?"

What it watches

The biggest health risks in deep space

Voice assistant

Ask out loud and hear the answer, hands-free. A friendly face can guide a calming breathing exercise.

Heart and vitals

Heart rate, temperature and blood pressure, live from the sensor.

Sleep and mood

Sleep debt over a week, mood, and a reaction test that measures tiredness honestly.

Crew cross-check

You rate yourself and a crewmate rates you. A big difference is flagged.

Radiation

A running career dose against NASA's 600 mSv limit, plus a solar storm alarm.

Bones and muscles

Bone can't be scanned in space, so Astrodoc tracks exercise, water and vitamin D.

Blood clots

Watches blood tests for the dangerous pattern found on the ISS, and for worsening trends.

Science

Built on NASA research

  • NASA Human Research Program: radiation, sleep, isolation and confinement
  • NASA OCHMO technical briefs: bone loss and radiation protection (NASA-STD-3001, 600 mSv career limit)
  • NASA Human Research Roadmap: risk of bone fracture
  • Marshall-Goebel K, et al. Jugular venous blood flow stasis and thrombosis during spaceflight. JAMA Network Open, 2019
  • Clinical lab reference ranges for clotting tests

Some limits are team proposals that a medical team must confirm. The full table is in the dashboard under "How colors are decided".
